[HEADING_2] Mold After Water Damage: Why Bleach Does Not Fix It
[NORMAL_TEXT] Bleach can lighten the color of mold on hard, non-porous surfaces like tile, but it does not reach mold roots inside porous materials like drywall and wood. The water in household bleach can actually feed regrowth inside the material. Painting over mold hides it for a while, but it grows back through the paint. Real mold removal means removing contaminated porous materials, cleaning hard surfaces properly and fixing the moisture source. For small spots on non-porous surfaces like shower tile, a household mold cleaner is fine. For anything larger than about 10 square feet, inside walls or in the attic, professional remediation is the safe choice.

[HEADING_2] Containment: Why It Matters
[NORMAL_TEXT] Without containment, disturbing mold releases spores into the air, and those spores settle in clean rooms, the HVAC system and furniture. Plastic sheeting, sealed doorways and negative air pressure with HEPA filtration keep the work area separate from the rest of the home. Air scrubbers run continuously, and technicians remove debris in sealed bags. Containment is what separates professional mold remediation from simply cleaning a wall.
[HEADING_2] Mold Remediation vs Mold Removal
[NORMAL_TEXT] You will often see both terms. Mold removal usually refers to cleaning or cutting out visible growth. Mold remediation means the full process: finding the cause, containing the area, removing contaminated materials, cleaning, drying and confirming the area is clean. Since mold spores exist everywhere, the goal is not to remove every spore but to bring mold back to normal background levels and fix the moisture problem so it does not return.
