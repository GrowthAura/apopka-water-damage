[HEADING_2] Why do mobile home floors go soft after a leak?
[NORMAL_TEXT] Many manufactured homes use particleboard or OSB subfloors that swell, lose strength and crumble when wet, so even a slow leak can leave floors soft within days or weeks.
[NORMAL_TEXT] Particleboard is made of wood chips and glue pressed together. When it absorbs water, the chips swell and the glue bond breaks down. Unlike plywood, it does not regain its strength when it dries. That is why a leak under a kitchen sink, a toilet wax ring or a water heater closet in a mobile home so often leads to a soft spot, a dip in the floor or a hole you can push through. Newer homes may use OSB or plywood, which hold up better but still need fast drying.
[HEADING_2] How Manufactured Homes Are Built, and Why It Matters
[NORMAL_TEXT] Understanding the structure helps explain why water damage spreads the way it does.
[HEADING_3] Floor System
[NORMAL_TEXT] The floor sits on steel I-beams and wood floor joists, with a subfloor of particleboard, OSB or plywood on top. Carpet, vinyl or LVP goes over the subfloor.
[HEADING_3] Belly Wrap (Bottom Board)
[NORMAL_TEXT] Under the floor joists is a fabric or plastic sheet called the belly wrap or bottom board. It holds the insulation in place and keeps animals and moisture out. It also catches water from leaks above.
[HEADING_3] Plumbing
[NORMAL_TEXT] Supply lines and drain lines run through the floor cavity, between the subfloor and belly wrap. A leak there may never show inside the home.
[HEADING_3] Ductwork
[NORMAL_TEXT] Many homes have a main heating and cooling duct running down the center of the floor cavity. Leaks can soak the duct insulation and grow mold that is blown through the home.
[HEADING_3] Skirting
[NORMAL_TEXT] Skirting around the base hides the crawl area under the home. It also hides sagging belly wrap, standing water and mold.
[HEADING_2] Belly Wrap Leak: The Hidden Problem
[NORMAL_TEXT] A belly wrap leak happens when water from a supply line, drain, water heater or AC drain collects in the bottom board instead of draining out. The fabric sags into a bulge filled with water, sometimes holding dozens of gallons. Signs include:
[NORMAL_TEXT] A sagging or bulging section of bottom board under the home.
[NORMAL_TEXT] Water dripping from the belly wrap.
[NORMAL_TEXT] A musty smell inside the home, especially near floor vents.
[NORMAL_TEXT] Soft spots or dips in the floor above.
[NORMAL_TEXT] Higher humidity inside the home and condensation on windows.
[NORMAL_TEXT] Rodents or insects under the home attracted by the moisture.
[NORMAL_TEXT] The water must be drained, the wet insulation removed, the floor cavity dried and the bottom board repaired or replaced. Leaving it leads to rotted subfloor and joists.

[HEADING_2] Mold Under Manufactured Homes
[NORMAL_TEXT] The space under a manufactured home is shaded, often damp, and closed in by skirting, which makes it a natural place for mold. When a leak soaks the belly insulation or ductwork, mold can grow on the underside of the subfloor, on joists and inside the duct insulation. Because the main duct often runs through the floor cavity, mold there can affect the air inside the home. Signs include a musty smell from floor vents, allergy symptoms that worsen when the AC runs, and visible growth on the bottom board. Remediation involves removing contaminated insulation and bottom board, cleaning and treating the framing, and checking or replacing affected duct sections.
