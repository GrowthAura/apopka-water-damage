[HEADING_2] Why do Apopka AC drain lines clog in summer?
[NORMAL_TEXT] Warm, humid air makes the AC pull gallons of condensate every day, and that constant moisture feeds algae and slime that block the drain line and overflow the pan.
[NORMAL_TEXT] In Apopka, air conditioners run for most of the year. On a humid summer day, a typical central system can remove 10 to 20 gallons of water from the air. That water drips off the evaporator coil into a drain pan and flows through a small PVC condensate line to the outside. Algae, dust, mold spores and insects build up inside that line. Once it clogs, the water has only one place to go: over the edge of the pan and into your home.

[HEADING_2] Clogged Condensate Line vs Other AC Leak Causes
[NORMAL_TEXT] Clogged condensate line: The most common cause. Algae and debris block the line and the pan overflows.
[NORMAL_TEXT] Rusted or cracked drain pan: Metal pans corrode in Florida's climate and leak through the bottom.
[NORMAL_TEXT] Failed float switch: The safety switch should turn off the AC when water rises. If it fails, the overflow continues.
[NORMAL_TEXT] Disconnected drain line: Fittings can slip apart, especially after recent HVAC work.
[NORMAL_TEXT] Frozen evaporator coil: Low refrigerant or a dirty filter freezes the coil. When it thaws, more water than the pan can handle drips out.
[NORMAL_TEXT] Dirty air filter: Restricts airflow, which can lead to coil freezing and overflow.
[NORMAL_TEXT] Condensate pump failure: Homes with a pump instead of a gravity line flood when the pump stops.

[NORMAL_TEXT] Yes. Turn it off at the thermostat and, if water is near the unit's electrical parts, at the breaker. This stops new condensate from forming.
