[HEADING_2] Why Slab Leaks Happen in Central Florida Homes
[NORMAL_TEXT] Age of the plumbing: Copper lines installed decades ago wear thin from the inside, especially where water chemistry is aggressive.
[NORMAL_TEXT] Abrasion: Pipes that rub against concrete, gravel or rebar wear through slowly with every pressure change and thermal expansion of hot lines.
[NORMAL_TEXT] Soil movement: Apopka's sandy soil and high water table shift with heavy rain and drought, putting stress on pipes below the slab. The area's porous limestone also allows the ground beneath some homes to settle over time.
[NORMAL_TEXT] High water pressure: Pressure above roughly 80 psi strains every joint in the system.
[NORMAL_TEXT] Poor original installation: Kinked lines, bad joints or pipes laid too close to sharp aggregate fail sooner.
[NORMAL_TEXT] Hot water lines: Most slab leaks occur on hot lines, because heating and cooling cycles make the pipe expand and contract daily.

[HEADING_2] How We Dry a Wet Slab
[NORMAL_TEXT] Concrete holds water like a sponge and gives it up slowly, especially in Apopka's humid air. Drying a slab properly takes more than fans.
[HEADING_3] 1. Moisture Mapping
[NORMAL_TEXT] Moisture meters and thermal imaging show exactly which parts of the slab and which walls are wet. The wet zone is often much larger than the visible damage.
[HEADING_3] 2. Flooring Assessment
[NORMAL_TEXT] Carpet and pad over a slab leak are usually removed in the wet area. Tile may be saved if the grout and thinset are sound. LVP and laminate are lifted to let the slab breathe. Engineered hardwood is assessed plank by plank.
[HEADING_3] 3. Baseboard and Drywall
[NORMAL_TEXT] Baseboards are removed in wet rooms, and drywall is cut only where moisture readings stay high. Small holes may be drilled to dry wall cavities without large cuts.
[HEADING_3] 4. Slab Drying
[NORMAL_TEXT] Commercial dehumidifiers, air movers and, when needed, heat drying pull moisture out of the concrete. Readings are taken every day until the slab reaches an acceptable level for new flooring.
[HEADING_3] 5. Antimicrobial Treatment
[NORMAL_TEXT] The slab surface, tack strip areas and wall bottoms are treated to prevent mold.
[HEADING_3] 6. Rebuild
[NORMAL_TEXT] New flooring, baseboard and drywall go in only after readings confirm everything is dry. Installing new LVP or tile over a damp slab traps moisture and leads to failed floors and mold.

[HEADING_2] High Water Bill Leak: Reading Your Meter
[NORMAL_TEXT] One of the easiest ways to confirm a hidden leak is a simple meter test you can do in a few minutes.
[NORMAL_TEXT] Turn off every faucet, shower, appliance, irrigation zone and ice maker in and around the house.
[NORMAL_TEXT] Find your water meter, usually in a box near the street or sidewalk.
[NORMAL_TEXT] Look for the small triangle or star-shaped leak indicator, or write down the reading.
[NORMAL_TEXT] Wait 30 minutes without using any water, then check again.
[NORMAL_TEXT] If the indicator spins or the reading changes, water is escaping somewhere. If the toilets are not running and there is no wet spot outside, a slab leak is a strong possibility. A high water bill leak is often the very first sign Apopka homeowners notice, weeks before the floors show anything.

[NORMAL_TEXT] A long-running leak can wash out soil beneath the slab, which may contribute to settling or cracks over time, especially on sandy soil. Catching leaks early limits that risk.
