[HEADING_2] Supply Line Burst vs Slow Leak
[NORMAL_TEXT] A supply line burst is loud and obvious: water sprays or pours at full pressure, and a room can flood quickly. Shut the fixture valve or the main and call right away.
[NORMAL_TEXT] A slow leak is the opposite. A dripping ice-maker line, a weeping dishwasher connection or a failing wax ring can go on for weeks. The damage is quieter but often worse: rotted subfloor, swollen cabinets, mold behind walls and stained ceilings. If you notice a musty smell, soft flooring near an appliance or a stain on the ceiling below a bathroom, have the area checked even if you do not see standing water. [HEADING_2] Gray Water vs Clean Water From Appliances
[NORMAL_TEXT] The kind of water matters as much as the amount.
[NORMAL_TEXT] Clean water (Category 1): Fresh water from supply lines, such as a burst washer fill hose, fridge water line or toilet supply line. It can usually be dried in place if handled quickly.
[NORMAL_TEXT] Gray water (Category 2): Water that has been used, such as washing machine drain water, dishwasher discharge or toilet bowl water without waste. It contains soap, food particles and bacteria. Carpet pad is replaced, and all surfaces are cleaned and treated.
[NORMAL_TEXT] Black water (Category 3): Toilet overflow with waste, or water that backs up from the drain or sewer line. Porous materials it touches are removed and the area is sanitized before drying.
[NORMAL_TEXT] Even clean water becomes gray water within a day or two in a warm Apopka home, which is why fast extraction matters.
