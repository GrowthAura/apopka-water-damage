Where is the main water shut-off on most Apopka homes?

On most Apopka homes the main shut-off sits where the water line enters the house, usually near the front hose bib, in the garage or beside the water heater.

If you cannot find it, the City of Apopka or Orange County Utilities meter box near the street also has a valve. A water meter key or a pair of pliers turns it a quarter turn to close. Homes on a private well have a shut-off near the pressure tank, often in the garage or a utility closet. Once the water is off, open the lowest faucet in the house to drain the remaining pressure, and switch off breakers for any room where water touches outlets.

The First 30 Minutes After a Pipe Bursts

What you do before the crew arrives can save hours of drying and hundreds of dollars in repairs.

  • Close the main valve, then open a low faucet to relieve pressure.
  • Turn off the water heater, gas or electric, so it does not run dry.
  • Cut power to wet rooms at the breaker panel.
  • Photograph the broken pipe, the water and every affected room.
  • Move furniture, electronics, rugs and paperwork out of the water.
  • Put towels at doorways to slow the spread into dry rooms.

  • Do not try to cut into drywall to find the leak yourself. Ceiling drywall full of water is heavy and can come down without warning.

Why Pipes Burst in Apopka Homes

Florida rarely freezes, so most burst pipes here have other causes. Knowing the cause helps us dry the right areas and helps you prevent the next one.

Aging Supply Lines in Established Neighborhoods

Homes built from the 1970s through the 1990s around Errol Estate, downtown and the Park Avenue area may still have original copper, CPVC or even polybutylene lines. Polybutylene, the gray plastic pipe common in that era, becomes brittle and splits at fittings. CPVC grows brittle with heat and age, especially in hot attics.

Pinhole Leaks in Copper

Water chemistry and age cause copper to develop tiny pinholes that spray inside walls for days before anyone notices.

Failed Fittings and Braided Supply Lines

Toilet, sink and appliance supply lines with plastic nuts crack under pressure. A single failed line under a vanity can flood a bathroom and the hallway outside it.

High Water Pressure

Without a working pressure-reducing valve, pressure spikes stress every joint in the house.

Attic Pipe Runs

Many Florida homes run water lines through the attic. When one fails, water comes straight through the ceiling into the rooms below.

Lines Under the Slab

When a pipe fails under the concrete, the water shows up as damp flooring or a warm spot rather than a spray. Read more: Slab Leak Water Damage in Apopka

Pipe Burst in Wall: How We Find and Dry It

A pipe burst in wall is the hardest kind of leak to see and one of the most damaging. Water runs down inside the cavity, soaks the insulation and pools on the bottom plate, out of sight.

Thermal Imaging and Moisture Meters

The crew scans walls and ceilings to map exactly how far water traveled. This keeps demolition to the smallest area possible.

Flood Cuts Only Where Needed

When drywall is saturated, the bottom 12 to 24 inches may be cut away so air can reach the framing. Dry, sound drywall stays in place.

Cavity Drying

Specialty injectors push dry air into wall cavities through small holes, often avoiding larger cuts altogether.

Cabinet and Vanity Drying

Toe kicks are removed and cabinet bases dried from inside so swollen particleboard does not trap moisture.

Daily Monitoring

Readings are logged every day until framing, drywall and subfloor reach dry standard. Drying usually takes three to five days.

Can It Happen Again? Prevention Tips for Apopka Homes

  • Know where your main shut-off is and make sure it turns easily.
  • Replace rubber washer hoses with braided stainless lines every five years.
  • Check under sinks and vanities monthly for drips or corrosion.
  • Have a plumber test your water pressure; aim for roughly 40 to 80 psi.
  • Ask about repiping if your home still has polybutylene lines.
  • Install a smart leak sensor or automatic shut-off valve near the water heater and washer.
  • Before leaving on a trip, shut off the main valve.

Signs a Pipe Is About to Fail

Many burst pipes give small warnings first. Catching them early turns an emergency into a simple repair.

  • A water bill that creeps up month after month with no change in use.
  • The sound of running water when every fixture is off.
  • The water meter dial moving when nothing is running.
  • Damp or discolored drywall below a bathroom or near the kitchen sink.
  • Rust, green corrosion or white mineral crust on visible copper fittings.
  • Low or sputtering water pressure at one fixture.
  • A musty smell inside a vanity or kitchen sink cabinet.
  • If you notice two or more of these, have a plumber check the lines and ask us to scan the walls for hidden moisture before it becomes a burst.

Polybutylene and CPVC in Apopka: Should You Repipe?

Polybutylene was widely used in Florida homes built in the late 1970s through the mid-1990s. It is gray, sometimes blue or black, and stamped PB2110. Over time, chlorine in municipal water breaks it down from the inside, and fittings fail without warning. Many insurers ask about it, and some will not write a policy on a home that still has it.

CPVC, the cream or tan plastic pipe common in later homes, is more durable but grows brittle with age and heat, especially in attics. It can crack when bumped during repairs or AC work.

If your Apopka home still has polybutylene, talk to a plumber about repiping. It is far less expensive than repeated water damage, and it can make your home easier to insure. If you are not sure what you have, look at the pipes coming out of the water heater or under a sink.

What Not to Do After a Burst Pipe

Do not turn the water back on to test the pipe before a plumber repairs it.

  • Do not use a household vacuum on standing water.
  • Do not walk on a sagging, wet ceiling area or stand directly under it.
  • Do not plug in fans or space heaters in wet rooms.
  • Do not close up walls or reinstall baseboards until moisture readings confirm the area is dry.
  • Do not throw away damaged items before photographing them for your claim.
