Is your Apopka home on septic or city sewer?

Many homes in central Apopka are on City of Apopka sewer, while many rural, north and older homes toward Zellwood, Plymouth and Kelly Park use private septic systems.

Knowing which you have changes what caused the backup and who fixes it. A quick way to check: look at your water and sewer bill. If you pay a sewer charge to the City of Apopka or Orange County Utilities, you are on public sewer. If you only pay for water, or you are on a private well, you likely have a septic tank and drain field in the yard. Septic homes often have a concrete or plastic lid in the yard and may have records of past tank pumping.

Signs of a Sewage or Septic Backup

  • Toilets gurgling, bubbling or draining slowly, especially when another fixture is used.
  • Water or waste coming up in the shower, tub or floor drain when the toilet is flushed or the washer drains.
  • Several drains slow at the same time, which points to the main line or septic system rather than a single clog.
  • A strong sewage odor inside the home or around the yard.
  • Soggy, unusually green or smelly ground over the drain field.
  • Water backing up from the cleanout pipe outside the home.
  • If you see waste water in the home, stop using all water immediately. Every flush, shower or load of laundry adds more sewage to the problem.

Septic Backup Cleanup Apopka FL: Why Septic Systems Back Up

Full Tank

Septic tanks need pumping every few years. When solids build up, they block the outlet and waste backs up into the house.

Saturated Drain Field

Apopka's high water table and heavy summer rain can saturate the drain field, so effluent has nowhere to go. Backups after big storms are very common in septic homes.

Clogged or Collapsed Lines

Roots, grease and non-flushable items block the pipe between the house and the tank. Older clay or cast-iron lines can crack or collapse.

Failed Pump or Alarm

Some systems use a lift pump. If the pump fails, the tank fills and backs up into the home.

Overuse

Running several loads of laundry, long showers and a dishwasher in a short time can overload a system that is already near capacity.

Cast Iron Drain Backup in Older Apopka Homes

Many homes built before the mid-1970s, including some around downtown, Errol Estate and older parts of the Park Avenue area, have cast-iron drain lines under the slab. Over decades, cast iron corrodes from the inside, scales up, cracks and can collapse. The first sign is often slow drains and recurring clogs, followed by a backup that brings sewage up through the lowest drain in the house. A plumber can scope the line with a camera to see its condition. If the pipe is failing, lining or replacement prevents repeat backups. In the meantime, the sewage that already came into the home needs proper black water cleanup.

Black Water Cleanup: What Makes It Different

Sewage is Category 3 water under the IICRC S500 standard. It contains bacteria, viruses and parasites that can cause serious illness. That means cleanup follows strict rules:

  • Technicians wear protective suits, gloves, boots and respirators.
  • The work area is isolated so contamination does not spread to clean rooms.
  • Porous materials that touched sewage, such as carpet, pad, drywall, insulation, baseboards and particleboard cabinets, are removed and disposed of.
  • Every remaining surface is cleaned, then disinfected with EPA-registered products.
  • Drying only begins after cleaning and disinfection, so contamination is not blown around the home.
  • Air scrubbers with HEPA filtration can be used to reduce odors and airborne contaminants.

Toilet Sewage Backup vs Clean Toilet Overflow

A toilet that overflows clean water from the tank, or bowl water without waste, is a different job than a toilet sewage backup. If water rises in the toilet when you run the washer or shower, or comes up in the tub, the problem is in the main drain or septic system, not the toilet itself.   • Do not walk through sewage; if you must, wear rubber boots and gloves.

Sewage Cleanup Apopka: Health Risks

Raw sewage can carry E. coli, salmonella, hepatitis A and other pathogens. Exposure can cause stomach illness, skin and eye infections and breathing problems. The risk is higher for young children, older adults, pregnant women and people with weakened immune systems. Sewage odors can also contain hydrogen sulfide, which is irritating at low levels. After a backup, it is usually safest for vulnerable family members to stay elsewhere until cleanup and disinfection are complete. Mold can also grow quickly on sewage-soaked materials. Preventing Septic and Sewer Backups

Pump your septic tank every three to five years, or as your septic company recommends.

  • Keep cars, sheds and heavy equipment off the drain field.
  • Spread out laundry over the week instead of doing many loads in one day, especially in rainy season.
  • Never flush wipes, paper towels, grease or feminine products, even if labeled flushable.
  • Have older cast-iron or clay lines scoped by a plumber every few years.
  • Ask about a backwater valve if your home has had repeat sewer backups.
  • Add a water backup endorsement to your insurance policy.

Sewage Backup After Heavy Rain in Apopka

Summer storms are the most common trigger for septic backups in Apopka. When several inches of rain fall in a short time, the drain field fills with groundwater and the septic tank has nowhere to send effluent. Homes on city sewer can also see backups during big storms if stormwater gets into the sewer system and overloads it. If your drains start gurgling during or after a storm, cut back on water use right away: delay laundry, keep showers short, avoid the dishwasher and flush only when necessary. That simple step often prevents a backup from reaching the living space.

Septic Drain Field Problems in Sandy Florida Soil

Apopka's sandy soil drains well when dry, but the high water table in many areas means the drain field can sit close to groundwater for months during the rainy season. Over time, drain fields can also clog with a layer of biological mat that slows absorption, especially if the tank was not pumped regularly. Roots from nearby trees can invade drain lines. When a drain field fails, backups become more frequent and the yard over the field may stay wet or smell. A septic company can inspect the tank and field and recommend pumping, repair or replacement.

What Can Be Saved After a Sewage Backup

  • Ceramic and porcelain tile, after cleaning and disinfection.
  • Concrete slabs and block walls, after cleaning, disinfecting and drying.
  • Bathroom fixtures like toilets, tubs and sinks, after cleaning.
  • Solid wood framing, after cleaning, disinfecting and drying.

Items that usually must be discarded include carpet and pad, drywall and insulation below the affected line, upholstered furniture, mattresses, particleboard cabinets and any porous belongings that soaked up sewage.

Protecting Your Family During Cleanup

Keep children and pets out of the contaminated area until cleanup is finished.

  • Wash hands thoroughly after any contact with affected areas.
  • Wash clothing worn in the area separately in hot water.
  • Throw out food and medicine that may have been exposed.
  • Keep the HVAC system off in affected rooms if possible so odors and contaminants are not spread.
  • Call a doctor if anyone develops stomach illness, fever or skin infections after exposure.

Frequently Asked Questions About Sewage Backups

Can I clean up a small sewage backup myself?

+

It is not recommended. Even small amounts of sewage can contaminate grout, subfloor and walls, and household cleaners do not disinfect porous materials.

Can carpet be cleaned after a sewage backup?

+

No. Carpet and pad touched by sewage should be removed and discarded for health reasons.

Who is responsible if the city sewer backs up into my home?

+

It depends on where the blockage is. Lines on your property are usually the owner's responsibility; the main in the street is typically the utility's. A plumber can help determine where the problem is.

Why did my septic back up after heavy rain?

+

A saturated drain field cannot absorb more water, so effluent backs up into the tank and house. This is common in Apopka during the summer rainy season.

How long does sewage cleanup take?

+

Removal and disinfection usually take one to two days, followed by two to five days of drying, depending on the size of the affected area.

Is it safe to stay home during sewage cleanup?

+

For small, contained backups, many families can stay in unaffected parts of the home. For larger backups, or if vulnerable people live there, staying elsewhere is safer.
