[HEADING_2] How Storms Get Water Into Apopka Homes
[NORMAL_TEXT] Central Florida storms are not just heavy rain. Strong gusts drive water sideways, and that is what finds the weak points in a home.
[HEADING_3] Missing or Lifted Shingles
[NORMAL_TEXT] High winds lift and tear shingles, exposing the underlayment and roof deck. Water then runs into the attic and onto the ceiling below.
[HEADING_3] Failed Flashing and Vent Boots
[NORMAL_TEXT] Flashing around plumbing vents, skylights, chimneys and roof-to-wall joints is a common leak point. Rubber vent boots crack in Florida sun and let water through during wind-driven rain.
[HEADING_3] Soffit and Ridge Vents
[NORMAL_TEXT] Wind-driven rain is forced up into soffit vents and through ridge vents, soaking attic insulation even when the roof covering is intact.
[HEADING_3] Windows, Doors and Sliders
[NORMAL_TEXT] Sliding glass doors and older windows let water in under pressure, especially on the side of the home facing the wind. Water runs down inside the wall cavity or across the floor.
[HEADING_3] Fallen Trees and Limbs
[NORMAL_TEXT] Live oaks and pines are common around Apopka. Falling limbs can puncture the roof and let in large amounts of water quickly.
[HEADING_3] Overwhelmed Gutters and Drainage
[NORMAL_TEXT] Clogged gutters overflow against fascia and walls, and poor yard drainage can push water toward the slab and under doors. Read more: Flood Damage Cleanup in Apopka (/flood-damage-cleanup/)
[HEADING_2] Emergency Roof Tarping and Board-Up
[NORMAL_TEXT] The first job after a storm is stopping more water from getting in. When the roof is damaged, emergency roof tarping covers the opening with a heavy-duty tarp secured to the roof deck until a roofer can make permanent repairs. Tarping and board-up protect your home and also show your insurer that you took reasonable steps to prevent further damage, which most Florida policies require.

[HEADING_2] Wind-Driven Rain Damage: Why It Is Easy to Miss
[NORMAL_TEXT] Wind-driven rain damage often leaves no obvious hole in the roof. Water gets forced through vents, under shingles and around window frames, then soaks insulation and wall cavities quietly. The first sign might be a faint stain days later or a musty smell a week after the storm. If your home took a strong storm, it is worth having the attic and exterior walls on the windward side checked with a moisture meter, even if you do not see a leak. Finding hidden moisture early is far cheaper than dealing with mold and rotted framing later.
[HEADING_2] Hurricane Water Damage Apopka: Flood vs Wind-Driven Water
[NORMAL_TEXT] It matters for your insurance where the water came from.
[NORMAL_TEXT] Wind-driven water: Rain that enters through roof damage, broken windows or openings created by the storm. This is typically handled under your homeowners or windstorm coverage, subject to your hurricane deductible.
[NORMAL_TEXT] Flood water: Rising water from the ground, such as overflowing retention ponds, lakes or street flooding, that enters at ground level. Standard homeowners policies usually exclude it; it is covered by a separate flood policy through the NFIP or a private flood insurer.
[NORMAL_TEXT] Many storms bring both. Clear documentation of where each type of water entered helps your adjuster sort out coverage. Read more: Water Damage Insurance Claim Help in Apopka (/water-damage-insurance-claim-help/)
