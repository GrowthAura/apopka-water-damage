How do you know you have a slab leak in Apopka?

Common signs are a warm spot on the floor, damp carpet or lifting tile with no visible source, the sound of running water, and an unexplained jump in your water bill.

Nearly every home in Apopka is built on a concrete slab, with water lines running either through the slab or beneath it. When one of those lines develops a pinhole or a cracked joint, the water has nowhere to go but up through the concrete and sideways into your floors and walls. Because Apopka homes have no basement or crawl space to catch it, a slab leak often shows up in the living space first, sometimes several rooms away from the actual pipe.

Slab Leak Warning Signs, Room by Room

Floors

A warm or hot area on tile or LVP, usually from a hot water line.

  • Carpet that feels damp or smells musty with no spill.
  • Tile that sounds hollow, cracks or pops loose.
  • LVP or laminate planks that cup, gap or lift at the seams.
  • Hardwood that buckles or crowns in one area.

Walls and Baseboards

  • Baseboards that swell, darken or separate from the wall near the floor.
  • Paint bubbling just above the baseboard.
  • A musty smell in one room that will not go away.

Plumbing and Utilities

  • A water bill that jumps with no change in use.
  • The water meter moving when every fixture is off.
  • The sound of water running inside walls or under the floor.
  • The water heater running constantly, a sign a hot line is leaking.
  • Low water pressure at faucets and showers.

Outside the Home

  • A wet patch of ground or unusually green grass along the foundation.
  • Cracks in the slab edge or along the garage floor.
  • If you notice two or more of these, call for a moisture check. Read more: Leak Detection in Apopka

Why Slab Leaks Happen in Central Florida Homes

Age of the plumbing: Copper lines installed decades ago wear thin from the inside, especially where water chemistry is aggressive.

Abrasion: Pipes that rub against concrete, gravel or rebar wear through slowly with every pressure change and thermal expansion of hot lines.

Soil movement: Apopka's sandy soil and high water table shift with heavy rain and drought, putting stress on pipes below the slab. The area's porous limestone also allows the ground beneath some homes to settle over time.

  • High water pressure: Pressure above roughly 80 psi strains every joint in the system.
  • Poor original installation: Kinked lines, bad joints or pipes laid too close to sharp aggregate fail sooner.
  • Hot water lines: Most slab leaks occur on hot lines, because heating and cooling cycles make the pipe expand and contract daily.

How We Dry a Wet Slab

Concrete holds water like a sponge and gives it up slowly, especially in Apopka's humid air. Drying a slab properly takes more than fans.

1. Moisture Mapping

Moisture meters and thermal imaging show exactly which parts of the slab and which walls are wet. The wet zone is often much larger than the visible damage.

2. Flooring Assessment

Carpet and pad over a slab leak are usually removed in the wet area. Tile may be saved if the grout and thinset are sound. LVP and laminate are lifted to let the slab breathe. Engineered hardwood is assessed plank by plank.

3. Baseboard and Drywall

Baseboards are removed in wet rooms, and drywall is cut only where moisture readings stay high. Small holes may be drilled to dry wall cavities without large cuts.

4. Slab Drying

Commercial dehumidifiers, air movers and, when needed, heat drying pull moisture out of the concrete. Readings are taken every day until the slab reaches an acceptable level for new flooring.

5. Antimicrobial Treatment

The slab surface, tack strip areas and wall bottoms are treated to prevent mold.

6. Rebuild

New flooring, baseboard and drywall go in only after readings confirm everything is dry. Installing new LVP or tile over a damp slab traps moisture and leads to failed floors and mold.

How Long Does It Take to Dry a Slab in Apopka?

Most slab leak drying jobs take four to seven days. A small, quickly caught leak may dry in three days, while a long-running leak that saturated several rooms can take more than a week. Humidity, the thickness of the slab, the type of flooring removed and how long the leak ran all affect the timeline. Daily readings tell us when it is truly dry, not a guess based on the calendar.

Flooring Over a Slab Leak: What Can Be Saved

Carpet: Can sometimes be dried and reinstalled if the leak was clean and caught early. Pad is almost always replaced.

High Water Bill Leak: Reading Your Meter

One of the easiest ways to confirm a hidden leak is a simple meter test you can do in a few minutes.

  • Turn off every faucet, shower, appliance, irrigation zone and ice maker in and around the house.
  • Find your water meter, usually in a box near the street or sidewalk.
  • Look for the small triangle or star-shaped leak indicator, or write down the reading.
  • Wait 30 minutes without using any water, then check again.

If the indicator spins or the reading changes, water is escaping somewhere. If the toilets are not running and there is no wet spot outside, a slab leak is a strong possibility. A high water bill leak is often the very first sign Apopka homeowners notice, weeks before the floors show anything.

Slab Leak, Wet Floor or Something Else?

Not every damp floor over a slab is a pipe leak. Other common causes in Apopka homes look similar but need different fixes:

  • AC condensate leaking from a hallway closet can spread under flooring and mimic a slab leak. Read more: AC Leak Water Damage in Apopka
  • Groundwater pushing up through a slab after heavy rain, especially on low lots and in areas with a high water table near Lake Apopka.
  • Exterior water entering at a slider or door threshold and wicking under the flooring.
  • Condensation under LVP in rooms where the AC runs very cold over a cool slab.
  • A shower pan or tub drain leaking into the slab beside a bathroom.
  • Moisture mapping and a plumber's pressure test together tell the difference, so you only pay for the repair you actually need.

Frequently Asked Questions About Slab Leaks

Is a warm spot on the floor always a slab leak?

+

Not always, but it is one of the most reliable signs of a hot water line leaking under the slab. Radiant heat from sun or appliances can also warm floors, so a moisture check confirms it.

Can a slab leak cause mold?

+

Yes. Water trapped under carpet, LVP or baseboards in Apopka's humidity creates ideal conditions for mold, often within a few days. Read more: Mold Remediation in Apopka

Can a slab leak damage my foundation?

+

A long-running leak can wash out soil beneath the slab, which may contribute to settling or cracks over time, especially on sandy soil. Catching leaks early limits that risk.

Should I turn off my water if I suspect a slab leak?

+

Yes. Shut the main valve to stop the flow, and turn off the water heater if you suspect a hot water line.

Do I need to move out during slab leak drying?

+

Usually not. Most families stay home while affected rooms dry, though the equipment is noisy and some rooms may be off limits for a few days.

What is the difference between a slab leak and a foundation leak?

+

People often use the terms interchangeably. Both describe water from pipes under or inside the concrete slab. Foundation leak signs and slab leak signs are the same: warm floors, damp flooring, high bills and running water sounds.
