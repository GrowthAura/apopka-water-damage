[HEADING_2] Does Florida homeowners insurance cover sudden water damage?
[NORMAL_TEXT] Most Florida homeowners policies cover sudden and accidental water damage, like a burst pipe or failed water heater, but often exclude long-term leaks, wear and tear, and rising flood water.
[NORMAL_TEXT] Every policy is different, so the declarations page and the policy wording decide what is covered. In general, the insurer is looking at whether the damage was sudden, whether it was caused by a covered event, and whether you took reasonable steps to prevent more damage once you knew about it. That last point is why fast mitigation matters for your claim as much as for your home. This page is general information, not legal or insurance advice; your agent or policy is the final word.

[HEADING_2] Step by Step: Filing Your Water Damage Claim
[HEADING_3] Step 1: Stop the Water and Stay Safe
[NORMAL_TEXT] Shut off the water, cut power to wet areas and get people and pets out of harm's way.
[HEADING_3] Step 2: Document Everything Before Cleanup
[NORMAL_TEXT] Take wide photos of each room, close-ups of damage, the source of the leak and the water level on walls. Take video walking through the home. Photograph damaged belongings before moving them. These claim photos are some of the most valuable evidence you will have.
[HEADING_3] Step 3: Prevent Further Damage
[NORMAL_TEXT] Your policy almost certainly requires you to take reasonable steps to protect the property. Call for emergency extraction and drying right away. Waiting for the adjuster before drying can lead to mold and can hurt your claim. Read more: Emergency Water Extraction in Apopka (/emergency-water-extraction/)
[HEADING_3] Step 4: Report the Claim
[NORMAL_TEXT] Call your insurer or agent, or file online, as soon as possible. Write down the claim number, the date and the name of everyone you talk to.
[HEADING_3] Step 5: Keep Damaged Materials or Samples
[NORMAL_TEXT] Ask your adjuster whether to keep samples of carpet, pad, drywall or the failed part, such as the burst hose or pipe section. Do not throw out the failed part until the adjuster says it is fine.
[HEADING_3] Step 6: Track Every Expense
[NORMAL_TEXT] Keep receipts for emergency repairs, hotel stays, meals out if you cannot use your kitchen, and anything you buy to protect the home.
[HEADING_3] Step 7: The Adjuster Visit
[NORMAL_TEXT] The adjuster inspects the damage, reviews documentation and estimates the claim. Have your photos, moisture readings, drying logs and scope ready.
[HEADING_3] Step 8: Review the Estimate

[HEADING_2] Florida Rules Apopka Homeowners Should Know
[HEADING_3] Assignment of Benefits
[NORMAL_TEXT] Florida law changed in recent years so that, for most homeowners policies issued from 2023 onward, contractors cannot take over your claim through an assignment of benefits. In practice, you as the policyholder file, manage and get paid on your claim, and you pay the contractor. That is why having clear documentation in your own hands matters.
[HEADING_3] Claim Deadlines
[NORMAL_TEXT] Florida law sets deadlines for reporting property claims, and they have been shortened in recent years. Report water damage to your insurer as soon as you discover it and do not wait to see how bad it gets. Check your policy for exact time limits.
[HEADING_3] Hurricane Deductibles
[NORMAL_TEXT] If water damage results from a named hurricane, a separate hurricane deductible may apply, often a percentage of your dwelling coverage.
[HEADING_3] Mitigation Duty
[NORMAL_TEXT] Florida policies generally require you to protect the property from further damage. Fast extraction and drying help meet that duty.

[HEADING_2] How to Photograph Water Damage for Your Claim
[NORMAL_TEXT] Good claim photos follow a simple pattern: wide, medium, close.
[NORMAL_TEXT] Wide shots: Stand in the doorway and capture the whole room, showing where the water is.
[NORMAL_TEXT] Medium shots: Show each wall, the floor and the ceiling with visible damage.
[NORMAL_TEXT] Close-ups: Capture water lines on walls, stains, swollen baseboards, lifted flooring and damaged belongings.
[NORMAL_TEXT] The source: Photograph the failed pipe, hose, water heater or roof damage.
[NORMAL_TEXT] Labels: Photograph model and serial numbers on damaged appliances and electronics.
[NORMAL_TEXT] Video: Walk through the whole home slowly, narrating what you see.
[NORMAL_TEXT] Keep the original photo files with their dates and times. Back them up to the cloud or email them to yourself.
[HEADING_2] Additional Living Expenses
[NORMAL_TEXT] If your home is not livable during repairs, many homeowners policies include additional living expenses, sometimes called loss of use. This can cover the extra cost of a hotel or rental, meals and other costs above your normal living expenses. Keep every receipt and ask your adjuster early what is covered and what limits apply. Sewage backups, large losses and homes with no working kitchen or bathroom are common situations where this coverage matters.
[HEADING_2] Mitigation vs Repairs: Two Parts of the Claim
[NORMAL_TEXT] Most water damage claims have two parts. Mitigation is the emergency work to stop and limit the damage: extraction, removal of wet materials, drying and cleaning. Repairs, or rebuild, put the home back the way it was: drywall, paint, flooring, cabinets and trim. Adjusters often review them separately. Mitigation should start right away; repairs follow once the home is dry and the claim scope is agreed. Keeping both parts clearly documented makes the process smoother.
[HEADING_2] Frequently Asked Questions About Water Damage Claims
[HEADING_3] Should I call my insurance company or the restoration company first?
[NORMAL_TEXT] Stop the water first, then call for emergency mitigation and report the claim as soon as you can. Mitigation should not wait for the adjuster.
[HEADING_3] Will my claim be denied if I start drying before the adjuster comes?
[NORMAL_TEXT] Taking reasonable steps to prevent further damage is usually required. Document everything first with photos and video, then start drying.
[HEADING_3] Does insurance pay for the plumber?
[NORMAL_TEXT] Usually the cost to repair the failed pipe or appliance is not covered, while the resulting water damage may be. Some policies cover the cost to access a pipe under a slab or in a wall.
[HEADING_3] How long does a water damage claim take?
[NORMAL_TEXT] Simple claims can be settled in a few weeks; complex or disputed claims take longer. Good documentation speeds things up.
[HEADING_3] Is mold covered by insurance in Florida?
[NORMAL_TEXT] Mold is often covered only if it results from a covered sudden water loss, and many policies cap mold coverage.
[HEADING_3] What if my claim is denied?
[NORMAL_TEXT] Ask for the denial in writing with the policy language cited, review your documentation, and consider talking to your agent, a public adjuster or an attorney.
