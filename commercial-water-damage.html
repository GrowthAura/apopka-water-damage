Business Flood Cleanup Apopka: Storm and Rising Water

When storm water rises around a commercial building, it enters through loading docks, storefront doors and low entrances. This water is Category 3 and needs removal of affected drywall, insulation and porous materials, plus thorough disinfection. Commercial properties often have flood coverage through a separate policy, and clear documentation of the water line, inventory and equipment is essential for that claim. Protecting Inventory, Equipment and Records

Inventory: Move stock off the floor onto dry racks or pallets. Separate wet and dry items. Photograph and list everything that got wet before discarding anything.

  • Electronics: Shut down and unplug computers, servers and equipment in wet areas. Do not turn wet electronics back on until they have been checked.
  • Paper records: Wet documents can often be saved by freezing them quickly and having them professionally dried.
  • Machinery: Production equipment exposed to water should be inspected by the manufacturer or a qualified technician before use.
  • Plants and growing stock: For nurseries, stock exposed to contaminated flood water may need to be evaluated before sale.

Water Damage at Plant Nurseries and Greenhouse Operations

Nursery and greenhouse properties around Apopka have a mix of buildings: greenhouses and shade houses, potting and propagation buildings, shipping and packing sheds, offices, coolers and equipment rooms. Water problems often start with irrigation main breaks, failed solenoid valves, overflowing retention areas after heavy rain, or roof leaks on packing and office buildings. The greenhouse itself is designed for water, but offices, break rooms, packing areas and electrical rooms are not. Fast extraction and drying in those spaces keep shipping on schedule and protect records, computers and equipment. Contaminated runoff carrying fertilizer or soil should be treated as Category 3 when it enters buildings.

Fire Sprinkler Water Damage

Accidental sprinkler activation or a broken sprinkler pipe can release a large amount of water very fast, often from above, soaking ceilings, walls, inventory and electronics. Sprinkler water that has sat in pipes for years is often dark and smelly and should be treated as contaminated. After shutting the system down, call the fire sprinkler contractor and your fire protection monitoring company. Ceiling tiles, wet insulation and drywall are removed as needed, and the building is dried quickly to prevent mold above ceilings, where it is easy to miss.

Flat Roof Leaks on Commercial Buildings

Many commercial buildings in Apopka have flat or low-slope roofs. Water ponds on these roofs after heavy rain and finds its way in at seams, roof drains, HVAC curbs and parapet walls. Leaks can travel along the deck before appearing inside, sometimes far from the source. Wet ceiling tiles, stained drywall and dripping light fixtures are the usual signs. Drying the ceiling cavity, insulation and anything below is important even after the roof is repaired.
