Does Florida homeowners insurance cover sudden water damage?

Most Florida homeowners policies cover sudden and accidental water damage, like a burst pipe or failed water heater, but often exclude long-term leaks, wear and tear, and rising flood water.

Every policy is different, so the declarations page and the policy wording decide what is covered. In general, the insurer is looking at whether the damage was sudden, whether it was caused by a covered event, and whether you took reasonable steps to prevent more damage once you knew about it. That last point is why fast mitigation matters for your claim as much as for your home. This page is general information, not legal or insurance advice; your agent or policy is the final word.

What Is Usually Covered vs Not Covered

Often Covered

  • Burst or broken supply pipes.
  • Sudden water heater failure.
  • Appliance hose or supply line failure.
  • AC overflow that happened suddenly.
  • Water from storm damage to the roof or windows.
  • Accidental overflow of a tub or toilet.

Often Excluded or Limited

  • Slow leaks that went on for weeks or months.
  • Damage from wear, tear, rot or lack of maintenance.
  • Rising flood water from outside, which needs a separate flood policy.
  • Sewer or drain backup, unless you have a water backup endorsement.
  • Mold, unless it results from a covered loss, and often subject to a cap.
  • The cost to repair the failed pipe, appliance or water heater itself.

Step by Step: Filing Your Water Damage Claim

Step 1: Stop the Water and Stay Safe

Shut off the water, cut power to wet areas and get people and pets out of harm's way.

Step 2: Document Everything Before Cleanup

Take wide photos of each room, close-ups of damage, the source of the leak and the water level on walls. Take video walking through the home. Photograph damaged belongings before moving them. These claim photos are some of the most valuable evidence you will have.

Step 3: Prevent Further Damage

Your policy almost certainly requires you to take reasonable steps to protect the property. Call for emergency extraction and drying right away. Waiting for the adjuster before drying can lead to mold and can hurt your claim. Read more: Emergency Water Extraction in Apopka

Step 4: Report the Claim

Call your insurer or agent, or file online, as soon as possible. Write down the claim number, the date and the name of everyone you talk to.

Step 5: Keep Damaged Materials or Samples

Ask your adjuster whether to keep samples of carpet, pad, drywall or the failed part, such as the burst hose or pipe section. Do not throw out the failed part until the adjuster says it is fine.

Step 6: Track Every Expense

Keep receipts for emergency repairs, hotel stays, meals out if you cannot use your kitchen, and anything you buy to protect the home.

Step 7: The Adjuster Visit

The adjuster inspects the damage, reviews documentation and estimates the claim. Have your photos, moisture readings, drying logs and scope ready.

Step 8: Review the Estimate

Compare the adjuster's estimate to the scope of work. If something is missing, ask questions in writing and provide supporting documentation.

Florida Rules Apopka Homeowners Should Know

Assignment of Benefits

Florida law changed in recent years so that, for most homeowners policies issued from 2023 onward, contractors cannot take over your claim through an assignment of benefits. In practice, you as the policyholder file, manage and get paid on your claim, and you pay the contractor. That is why having clear documentation in your own hands matters.

Claim Deadlines

Florida law sets deadlines for reporting property claims, and they have been shortened in recent years. Report water damage to your insurer as soon as you discover it and do not wait to see how bad it gets. Check your policy for exact time limits.

Hurricane Deductibles

If water damage results from a named hurricane, a separate hurricane deductible may apply, often a percentage of your dwelling coverage.

Mitigation Duty

Florida policies generally require you to protect the property from further damage. Fast extraction and drying help meet that duty.

If you have a Citizens policy, read your declarations page and policy carefully, report the loss promptly, document everything and ask your adjuster how mitigation and repairs will be handled for your specific policy. Condo, HOA and Rental Claims

Water damage in townhomes, condos and rentals often involves more than one policy. In a condo or townhome, the association's master policy may cover parts of the building while your HO-6 policy covers interior finishes and belongings, depending on your association documents and Florida law. In a rental, the landlord's policy usually covers the building and the tenant's renters policy covers belongings. When water comes from a neighbor's unit, both policies may be involved. Common Mistakes That Hurt Water Damage Claims

  • Waiting days to start drying, letting mold grow.
  • Throwing away damaged items before photographing them.
  • Discarding the failed pipe, hose or part before the adjuster sees it.
  • Painting over stains or replacing drywall before documentation.
  • Not reporting the claim promptly.
  • Not keeping receipts for emergency expenses.
  • Trying to dry a large loss with household fans and no moisture readings.
  • Agreeing to a settlement without comparing it to the full scope of work.

How to Photograph Water Damage for Your Claim

  • Good claim photos follow a simple pattern: wide, medium, close.
  • Wide shots: Stand in the doorway and capture the whole room, showing where the water is.
  • Medium shots: Show each wall, the floor and the ceiling with visible damage.
  • Close-ups: Capture water lines on walls, stains, swollen baseboards, lifted flooring and damaged belongings.
  • The source: Photograph the failed pipe, hose, water heater or roof damage.
  • Labels: Photograph model and serial numbers on damaged appliances and electronics.
  • Video: Walk through the whole home slowly, narrating what you see.
  • Keep the original photo files with their dates and times. Back them up to the cloud or email them to yourself.

Additional Living Expenses

If your home is not livable during repairs, many homeowners policies include additional living expenses, sometimes called loss of use. This can cover the extra cost of a hotel or rental, meals and other costs above your normal living expenses. Keep every receipt and ask your adjuster early what is covered and what limits apply. Sewage backups, large losses and homes with no working kitchen or bathroom are common situations where this coverage matters.

Mitigation vs Repairs: Two Parts of the Claim

Most water damage claims have two parts. Mitigation is the emergency work to stop and limit the damage: extraction, removal of wet materials, drying and cleaning. Repairs, or rebuild, put the home back the way it was: drywall, paint, flooring, cabinets and trim. Adjusters often review them separately. Mitigation should start right away; repairs follow once the home is dry and the claim scope is agreed. Keeping both parts clearly documented makes the process smoother.

Frequently Asked Questions About Water Damage Claims

Should I call my insurance company or the restoration company first?

+

Stop the water first, then call for emergency mitigation and report the claim as soon as you can. Mitigation should not wait for the adjuster.

Will my claim be denied if I start drying before the adjuster comes?

+

Taking reasonable steps to prevent further damage is usually required. Document everything first with photos and video, then start drying.

Does insurance pay for the plumber?

+

Usually the cost to repair the failed pipe or appliance is not covered, while the resulting water damage may be. Some policies cover the cost to access a pipe under a slab or in a wall.

How long does a water damage claim take?

+

Simple claims can be settled in a few weeks; complex or disputed claims take longer. Good documentation speeds things up.

Is mold covered by insurance in Florida?

+

Mold is often covered only if it results from a covered sudden water loss, and many policies cap mold coverage.

What if my claim is denied?

+

Ask for the denial in writing with the policy language cited, review your documentation, and consider talking to your agent, a public adjuster or an attorney.
