Apopka sits inland, away from storm surge, but hurricanes and tropical storms still bring damaging wind, sideways rain, tornadoes and flooding as they cross Central Florida. Most hurricane damage inside homes here is water damage: roof leaks, water forced through windows and sliders, and rising water in low areas. Use this hurricane water damage checklist for Florida homes to prepare before a storm and act fast after it.

Where can Apopka residents get sandbags before a storm?

Orange County and the City of Apopka typically open sandbag distribution sites before tropical storms and hurricanes. Check official county and city announcements for locations and times.

Sandbag sites usually open a day or two before a storm is expected, and supplies can run out quickly. Bring a shovel and gloves, since many sites are self-fill. Place sandbags in front of garage doors, sliding glass doors and low entry points where water collects. Sandbags slow water down but do not make a home waterproof.

Hurricane Prep Water Damage Florida: Before Storm Season (Spring)

Roof and Gutters

Have the roof inspected for missing or loose shingles, damaged flashing and cracked vent boots.

  • Clean gutters and downspouts, and extend downspouts away from the foundation.
  • Trim tree limbs that hang over the roof, especially large oaks and pines.

Windows, Doors and Sliders

  • Check caulk and weatherstripping around windows and doors.
  • Clear the weep holes and tracks on sliding glass doors.
  • Make sure shutters or impact panels are in good shape and easy to install.

Plumbing and AC

  • Flush the AC condensate line and test the float switch.
  • Know where your main water shut-off is and make sure it turns easily.

Documents and Insurance

  • Review your homeowners policy, hurricane deductible and whether you have flood coverage.
  • Consider flood insurance; policies usually have a waiting period before they take effect.
  • Take photos and video of every room and your belongings for a home inventory.
  • Store insurance documents, IDs and important papers in a waterproof container or digitally.

When a Storm Is 3 to 5 Days Away

  • Buy tarps, plastic sheeting, duct tape, buckets, towels and a wet/dry vacuum.
  • Get sandbags if your home or garage is in a low area.
  • Fill prescriptions and stock water, food and batteries.
  • Charge phones and power banks.
  • Test your generator outdoors and stock fuel safely.
  • Save the number for emergency water damage help: (407) 787-9139.

When a Storm Is 1 to 2 Days Away

  • Install shutters or impact panels.
  • Bring in patio furniture, grills, plants and anything that can blow away.
  • Place sandbags at garage doors and sliders.
  • Move valuables, electronics and documents off the floor and away from windows.
  • Roll up area rugs in rooms near sliders and exterior doors.
  • Turn the fridge and freezer to the coldest settings.
  • Unplug electronics you will not need.

During the Storm

  • Stay inside, away from windows and sliders.
  • Put towels at the base of doors and sliders where water may be forced in.
  • Watch ceilings for stains or drips and catch water with buckets.
  • If water is near outlets, turn off that circuit at the breaker if it is safe to do so.
  • Never use a generator indoors, in the garage or near open windows.

Storm Cleanup After Hurricane: The First 48 Hours

Safety First

  • Wait for the all-clear from officials before going outside.
  • Stay away from downed power lines and standing water near them.
  • Check for gas smells; leave and call the gas company if you smell gas.

Document the Damage

  • Photograph the outside of the home, the roof if visible from the ground, and every room with damage before cleanup.
  • Photograph damaged belongings and keep any damaged parts or materials until your adjuster says otherwise.

Stop More Water

  • Get an emergency tarp on the roof if it is damaged. Do not climb on a wet roof yourself.
  • Board up broken windows and doors.

Start Drying

  • Extract standing water, remove wet rugs and move furniture.
  • Water inside walls, attics and under flooring needs professional drying to prevent mold in Florida's humidity. Read more: Storm and Roof Leak Water Damage in Apopka

Report Your Claim

  • Call your insurer, get a claim number and keep notes of every conversation.
  • Keep receipts for tarps, supplies, hotel stays and meals.

Wind-Driven Rain vs Flood Water After a Hurricane

Wind-driven rain that enters through roof damage, broken windows or openings caused by the storm is usually handled under homeowners or windstorm coverage, subject to your hurricane deductible. Rising flood water that enters from the ground is usually covered only by a separate flood policy. Document where water came in, because it affects which policy applies. Read more: Flood Damage Cleanup in Apopka

Generator Safety in a Wet House

Run generators only outdoors, at least 20 feet from doors, windows and vents.

  • Never plug a generator into a wall outlet; use heavy-duty extension cords or a transfer switch installed by an electrician.
  • Keep the generator dry, under an open canopy, not in standing water.
  • Do not use electrical equipment while standing in water.
  • Install battery-powered carbon monoxide alarms.

Mold After a Hurricane

After a storm, power outages mean no AC, and homes in Apopka can become hot and humid fast. Combined with any water intrusion, mold can grow within 24 to 48 hours. If water got into walls, ceilings or under flooring, get it dried professionally as soon as possible. If you notice a musty smell or spots after the storm, read more: Mold Remediation in Apopka

Special Notes for Apopka Homes

Septic Homes

If your home is on septic, cut back on water use during and right after heavy rain. A flooded drain field can push sewage back into the house.

Manufactured Homes

Check tie-downs before the season, secure skirting, and after the storm look under the home for standing water or sagging bottom board.

Pool Homes

Do not drain your pool before a storm; a lowered water level can damage the shell. Lower it only slightly if your pool contractor recommends it, and turn off pool equipment.

Homes With Attic Air Handlers

After power returns, check the drain pan and condensate line before running the AC for long periods. Debris and power cycling can cause overflows.
