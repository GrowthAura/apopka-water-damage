[HEADING_2] What Happens During a Mold Inspection
[HEADING_3] Visual Inspection
[NORMAL_TEXT] The inspector walks the home, checking AC closets, bathrooms, under sinks, behind appliances, around windows and sliders, in the garage and in the attic for visible growth, staining and water damage.
[HEADING_3] Moisture Mapping
[NORMAL_TEXT] Moisture meters measure water content in drywall, baseboards, wood and flooring. Thermal imaging shows temperature differences that often reveal hidden wet areas behind walls and ceilings. Mold cannot grow without moisture, so moisture mapping is the most useful part of the inspection.
[HEADING_3] Humidity Readings
[NORMAL_TEXT] Indoor relative humidity and temperature are measured in different rooms. Readings consistently above about 60 percent create conditions where mold can grow even without a leak.
[HEADING_3] Air Sampling
[NORMAL_TEXT] Air samples are collected inside and outside the home and sent to a lab. Comparing indoor and outdoor spore types and counts shows whether there is an indoor source of mold.
[HEADING_3] Surface Sampling
[NORMAL_TEXT] Tape lifts or swabs of suspicious spots identify whether a stain is mold and what type.
[HEADING_3] Report
[NORMAL_TEXT] You receive a summary of findings, photos, moisture readings, lab results and recommendations, including where remediation is needed and what moisture problem must be fixed.
[HEADING_2] Mold Testing Apopka FL: Air vs Surface Samples
[NORMAL_TEXT] Air samples: Best for detecting hidden mold and comparing indoor air to outdoor air. Useful when you smell mold but cannot see it.
[NORMAL_TEXT] Surface samples: Best for confirming whether a visible spot is mold and identifying the type. Fast and simple.
[NORMAL_TEXT] Wall cavity samples: Taken through a small hole in drywall to check for growth inside a wall without opening it up.
[NORMAL_TEXT] Not every situation needs lab testing. If mold is clearly visible and the moisture source is known, the money is often better spent on fixing the problem. Testing is most valuable when mold is suspected but not visible, after remediation, or for real estate and legal documentation.

[HEADING_2] Air Quality Test for Mold: Can You DIY It?
[NORMAL_TEXT] Home mold test kits sold in stores usually use a petri dish left open in a room. Because mold spores are present in nearly all indoor air, these kits almost always grow something and cannot tell you whether you have an indoor mold problem. They also do not compare indoor and outdoor levels or locate the source. A professional inspection combines moisture mapping, visual checks and properly collected samples analyzed by an accredited lab, which gives you answers you can act on.
