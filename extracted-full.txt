[HEADING_2] Water Damage Categories: Clean, Gray and Black Water
The IICRC S500 standard sorts water losses into three categories, and the category decides how your home is cleaned.
Category 1, clean water: Comes from a sanitary source such as a burst supply pipe, water heater or ice-maker line. Usually the fastest to dry if handled quickly.
Category 2, gray water: Contains some contamination, such as washing machine discharge, dishwasher overflow or AC condensate that has sat in a pan. Needs cleaning and antimicrobial treatment.
Category 3, black water: Sewage, septic backups and outdoor flood water. Porous materials it touches usually have to be removed, and the area must be sanitized before drying.
Clean water does not stay clean. After about 48 hours in a warm Apopka home, Category 1 water can degrade into Category 2 or 3, which is one more reason to call right away.

[HEADING_1] How to Keep Your AC Drain Line From Flooding Your Florida Home
In Central Florida, your air conditioner removes gallons of water from the air every day. All of it has to leave through one small PVC pipe. When that condensate line clogs, the water ends up in your closet, hallway or ceiling instead. Here is how to unclog an AC drain line in Florida, keep it clear, and spot trouble before it turns into water damage.
[HEADING_2] How often should an Apopka AC drain line be flushed?
Flush it every one to three months during cooling season, and monthly in the humid summer, because Central Florida heat and moisture grow algae in the line quickly.
In Apopka, cooling season lasts most of the year. Warm, wet conditions inside the drain line are perfect for algae, mold and bacteria, which form a slimy clog. A quick flush with white vinegar on a regular schedule keeps that buildup from blocking the line.
[HEADING_2] How Your AC Drain System Works
When warm, humid air passes over the cold evaporator coil inside your air handler, moisture condenses on the coil, the same way water forms on a cold glass. That water drips into a primary drain pan and flows out through a condensate drain line, usually 3/4-inch PVC, to the outside of the home or into a plumbing drain.
Most systems also have:
A cleanout access tee, a short vertical pipe with a cap near the air handler for flushing the line.
A float switch that shuts off the AC if water rises in the pan.
A secondary or emergency drain pan under attic units.
An emergency drain line that often drips over a window or soffit to warn you the main line is clogged.
[HEADING_2] Signs Your AC Drain Line Is Clogged
Water in the primary or secondary drain pan.
The AC shutting off by itself, a sign the float switch tripped.
Water dripping from a pipe above a window or soffit outside.
A musty smell near the air handler or vents.
Damp carpet or baseboards near the AC closet.
A ceiling stain under an attic air handler.
Gurgling or no water coming from the outside drain line end while the AC runs.
[HEADING_2] How to Unclog an AC Drain Line in Florida: Step by Step
[HEADING_3] Step 1: Turn Off the AC
Switch the system off at the thermostat. For safety, also turn off the breaker for the air handler.
[HEADING_3] Step 2: Find the Cleanout

[HEADING_3] Step 3: Clear the Outside End

[HEADING_3] Step 4: Flush With Vinegar

[HEADING_3] Step 5: Rinse

[HEADING_3] Step 6: Check the Pan

[HEADING_1] How Fast Does Mold Grow After Water Damage in Florida?
After a leak, the clock starts right away. In Florida's warm, humid climate, mold can begin growing on wet materials within 24 to 48 hours, and visible growth often appears within one to two weeks. Here is what happens hour by hour, the early signs of mold after water damage, and how to stop it before it starts.
[HEADING_2] Can mold grow in 24 hours in Apopka's humidity?
Yes. Under the right conditions, mold spores on wet drywall, carpet pad or wood can begin to germinate within 24 to 48 hours, especially in warm, humid homes like those in Apopka.
Mold spores are already present in nearly every home. They only need three things to grow: moisture, food and the right temperature. Drywall paper, wood, carpet backing, dust and cardboard are all food. Apopka homes are warm most of the year, and outdoor humidity is high, so once materials are wet, moisture is the only missing ingredient. That is why the first two days after a leak matter so much.
[HEADING_2] The Mold Timeline After Water Damage
[HEADING_3] First Few Hours
Water spreads across floors, wicks up drywall and soaks into carpet pad and baseboards. Extraction during this window gives the best chance of saving materials.
[HEADING_3] 24 to 48 Hours
Mold spores on damp surfaces begin to germinate. You will not see anything yet, but growth is starting, especially in dark, warm spaces like closets, behind baseboards and under carpet.
[HEADING_3] 3 to 7 Days
Mold colonies can become established. A musty odor often appears. Clean water from a supply line can turn into contaminated water as bacteria multiply. Drywall and particleboard begin to break down.
[HEADING_3] 1 to 2 Weeks
Visible spots can appear on drywall, baseboards and the back of carpet. Mold may spread inside wall cavities and into insulation.
[HEADING_3] Weeks to Months
Long-term moisture leads to extensive growth, wood rot and damage to framing. Mold may spread to other areas through air movement and the HVAC system.
[HEADING_2] Why Mold Grows Faster in Florida
High humidity: Outdoor humidity often stays above 70 percent, slowing evaporation and keeping materials damp.
Warm temperatures: Mold grows fastest between about 70 and 90 degrees, which describes most Florida homes.
AC condensate: Air conditioners run most of the year and produce constant moisture in closets and attics.
Concrete slabs: Slabs hold water and release it slowly, keeping flooring and baseboards damp.
Storms and power outages: Hurricanes and storms bring water in, and outages shut off AC, raising indoor humidity fast.
[HEADING_2] Signs of Mold After Water Damage
A musty or earthy smell that does not go away.
Dark spots or fuzzy growth on drywall, baseboards or ceilings.
Paint that bubbles, peels or discolors after a leak.
Carpet that smells sour after drying.
Warped or soft drywall at the bottom of walls.
Allergy symptoms that worsen at home.
Mold behind baseboards is especially common after slab leaks and AC overflows, because the back of the baseboard and the bottom of the drywall stay wet long after the floor looks dry.
[HEADING_2] Florida Humidity Mold Prevention: What to Do After a Leak
Stop the source of water as quickly as possible.
Extract standing water within the first few hours.
Remove wet carpet pad, rugs and anything that soaked up water.
Run the AC to lower indoor humidity; aim for 40 to 50 percent.
Use dehumidifiers, not just fans; fans alone cannot remove moisture from humid air.
Check moisture in walls and under flooring with a meter, not just by touch.
Call for professional drying if more than a small area got wet, if water got into walls, or if it has been more than a day.

Bleach can clean mold from hard, non-porous surfaces like tile and glass, but it does not soak into porous materials like drywall and wood where mold roots grow. It also adds water. Mold cannot grow on dry materials.
