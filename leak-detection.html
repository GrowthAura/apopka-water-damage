Why is my Apopka water bill suddenly high?

A sudden jump usually means a running toilet, an irrigation leak, a dripping fixture or a hidden leak in a wall or under the slab. A meter test quickly shows which.

Before anything else, check the simple causes. A running toilet can waste hundreds of gallons a day. A broken irrigation head or line in the yard can run every time the system turns on. A dripping outdoor hose bib adds up. If none of those explain it, turn off every fixture and look at your water meter. If the leak indicator moves, water is escaping somewhere you cannot see. City of Apopka and Orange County Utilities customers can also compare monthly usage on their bills to spot when the increase started.

How Professional Leak Detection Works

Thermal Imaging

A thermal imaging leak scan shows temperature differences on walls, floors and ceilings. Water evaporating from a wet area is cooler than dry surroundings, and hot water lines leaking under a slab show up as warm spots. Thermal imaging points to where to look closer, without opening anything.

Moisture Meters

Pin and pinless meters measure water content in drywall, baseboards, wood and flooring to confirm and map wet areas.

Acoustic Listening

Sensitive listening equipment picks up the sound of water escaping from a pressurized pipe, even through concrete or behind tile.

Pressure Testing

Sections of the plumbing system are isolated and pressurized to see which line is losing pressure, narrowing down the location.

Meter and Fixture Isolation

Shutting off fixtures one by one and watching the meter rules out toilets, irrigation and appliances.

Visual Inspection and Cameras

Under sinks, behind access panels, in the attic and around the water heater. Borescope cameras can look inside wall cavities through a small hole.

DIY Leak Checks Before You Call

Check the meter: turn off all water, watch the leak indicator for 15 to 30 minutes.

  • Toilet dye test: add food coloring to the tank, wait 15 minutes without flushing. Color in the bowl means the flapper is leaking.
  • Check under sinks and around the water heater for drips or rust.
  • Walk the yard looking for wet spots, especially between the meter and the house.
  • Run each irrigation zone and look for broken heads or pooling water.
  • Feel floors for warm spots, which can mean a hot water slab leak.
  • If the meter moves and you have ruled out toilets and irrigation, it is time for professional leak detection.

Why Fast Leak Detection Matters in Florida

In Apopka's warm, humid climate, a hidden leak does more than waste water. Wet materials inside walls and under floors can grow mold within 24 to 48 hours, wood framing can start to rot, and particleboard cabinets and subfloors can swell beyond repair. Termites are also drawn to damp wood. The sooner a leak is found, the less needs to be removed and replaced.

Irrigation and Outdoor Leaks in Apopka Yards

Most Apopka homes have in-ground irrigation systems, and they are one of the most common hidden sources of a high water bill. Broken heads, cracked lines from roots or lawn equipment, and valves that do not close fully can waste large amounts of water. Some homes in Apopka use reclaimed water for irrigation, which is metered separately, so check which meter is showing the extra use. Signs of an irrigation leak include soggy spots in the lawn, water running down the curb, patches of unusually green grass and sinkholes or depressions along the line. Running each zone while watching for leaks usually finds the problem.

Shower Pan and Tub Leaks

A leaking shower pan or tub drain is a common hidden leak in Florida homes. Because showers sit on the slab, water from a failed pan seeps into the slab and the walls around the shower, often showing up as damp carpet or swollen baseboards in the bedroom or closet next door. A simple test is to plug the drain and fill the shower base with an inch or two of water, mark the level and check it after several hours. A dropping level points to a pan leak. Grout and caulk failures around tile can also let water behind the walls.

Pool Leaks and Water Bills

Homes with pools sometimes see high water bills because the pool auto-fill keeps adding water to replace a leak. Losing more than about a quarter inch per day beyond normal evaporation is a sign of a leak. A bucket test, placing a bucket of water on the pool step and comparing the water levels after a day, helps tell evaporation from a leak. Pool leaks are handled by pool leak specialists, but if water from a pool leak is reaching the home's slab or walls, the moisture should be checked.

Frequently Asked Questions About Leak Detection

Can a leak be found without tearing out walls?

+

Usually yes. Thermal imaging, moisture meters and acoustic equipment locate most leaks with little or no damage. A small access hole may be needed to confirm.

How long does leak detection take?

+

Most homes take one to three hours. Slab leaks and multiple leaks can take longer.

Can thermal imaging see through walls?

+

Not exactly. It shows surface temperature differences, which often reveal moisture or hot water lines behind walls and under floors.

Do you repair the pipe after finding the leak?

+

The pipe repair is done by a plumber. Leak detection pinpoints the location, and drying and repairs to damaged materials can follow.

Is a leak in the yard my responsibility?

+

Usually, the line from the meter to the house is the homeowner's responsibility, while the meter and the line on the street side belong to the utility.
