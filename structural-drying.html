How long does it take to dry a wet house in Apopka?

Most homes reach dry standard in three to five days with commercial equipment, though slabs, hardwood and long-running leaks in Apopka's humidity can take seven days or more.

Drying time depends on how much water got in, how long it sat, what materials are wet and the conditions inside the home. Central Florida's humidity slows natural evaporation, which is why open windows and household fans rarely work here. A professional drying system controls the air in the affected rooms so moisture moves out of materials and into the dehumidifiers, where it is pumped away. Daily readings show exactly when each material has reached its dry goal.

The Science of Drying, Simply Explained

Drying is about moving water from wet materials into the air, then out of the air and out of the house. Four things control how fast that happens:

Airflow

Air movers blow across wet surfaces to speed evaporation. They are angled along walls and under cabinets to reach the places water hides.

Humidity

Commercial low-grain refrigerant (LGR) and desiccant dehumidifiers pull the evaporated water out of the air. Without them, humid Apopka air just puts moisture back into the materials.

Temperature

Warm air holds more moisture, so a controlled temperature speeds drying. Too cold and drying stalls; too hot without dehumidification and moisture moves into dry areas.

Time

Even with the right equipment, dense materials like concrete and hardwood release moisture slowly. Pulling equipment too early is the most common reason drying jobs fail.

Water Damage Drying Apopka FL: Equipment We Use

  • High-velocity air movers and axial fans to create airflow over wet surfaces.
  • Low-grain refrigerant dehumidifiers that pull large amounts of water from humid air.
  • Desiccant dehumidifiers for hardwood floors and difficult drying in cooler conditions.
  • Injectidry and wall cavity drying systems that push dry air inside walls through small holes.

  • HEPA air scrubbers when contamination or mold is a concern.
  • Pin and pinless moisture meters, thermo-hygrometers and thermal imaging cameras for readings.

Moisture Readings: How We Know It Is Dry

Every drying job starts by taking readings of unaffected materials in the home, such as a dry wall in another room. That sets the dry goal for each material. Then wet materials are read every day and logged.

  • Drywall is dry when it matches the readings of unaffected drywall in the same home.
  • Wood framing and subfloor are dry when they reach a stable reading close to the dry goal.
  • Concrete slabs are tested before new flooring is installed, since flooring manufacturers have their own moisture limits.
  • Relative humidity and temperature are logged in the drying area and outside the home.
  • Those daily moisture readings become part of your file, showing your insurer and future buyers that the home was dried properly.

Wet Drywall Drying: Save or Replace?

Drywall can often be dried in place when the water was clean, extraction started quickly and the drywall is still firm. Drywall usually needs to be replaced when:

  • It was touched by Category 2 or Category 3 water, such as sewage or flood water.
  • It is soft, crumbling, sagging or delaminating.
  • It has visible mold growth.
  • Water has been in it for several days.
  • It has insulation behind it that is soaked and holding water against the drywall.
  • When drywall is kept, small holes are drilled at the bottom behind the baseboard so dry air can be pushed into the cavity. The holes are hidden once the baseboard goes back on.

Commercial Dehumidifier Rental vs Pro Drying

It is possible to rent a dehumidifier and fans, and for a very small, clean spill caught right away, that may be enough. For anything larger, there are real differences:

  • Rental units are often smaller and less efficient in Florida's humidity.
  • Without moisture meters, there is no way to know when walls, framing or the slab are actually dry.
  • Water hidden inside walls, under cabinets and under flooring is easy to miss without thermal imaging.
  • Drying logs and readings are what insurance adjusters ask for; a rental gives you none.
  • If drying fails, mold growth inside walls can cost much more than professional drying would have.
  • Pro drying is a system: the right number of air movers and dehumidifiers for the room size and water load, placed correctly, adjusted daily and removed only when readings say it is time.

Can You Live in the Home During Drying?

In most cases, yes. Drying equipment is noisy and produces some heat, and you may need to keep certain rooms closed off. A few tips:

  • Keep doors to drying areas closed so the dehumidifiers work efficiently.
  • Do not turn equipment off or move it; that resets drying progress.
  • Keep the AC running to help control overall humidity in the home.
  • Keep children and pets away from cords and fans.
  • Let the crew in each day for readings and adjustments.

Drying Plans for Common Apopka Losses

AC Closet Overflow

Air movers are aimed into the closet and along the walls of adjoining rooms, the closet platform is opened if needed, and a dehumidifier runs in the hallway. Most AC closet jobs dry in two to four days.

Kitchen Supply Line Leak

Toe kicks come off, air is directed under the cabinets, and wall cavity drying is used behind the sink and dishwasher. Slab and cabinet bases are monitored until dry.

Water Heater Failure in a Garage

The garage slab is squeegeed and dried, the bottom of the drywall is opened or drilled, and the interior wall shared with the house is checked and dried.

Slab Leak Under Flooring

Flooring is lifted in the wet area, the slab gets dehumidification and sometimes heat, and readings are taken until the concrete is ready for new flooring.

Attic Leak Through a Ceiling

Wet insulation is removed, air movers and a dehumidifier are placed in the attic or room below, and ceiling drywall is either dried or replaced.

Why Equipment Placement Matters

Drying is not about putting as many fans in a room as possible. Too many air movers without enough dehumidification just raise humidity and can push moisture into dry areas. Too few leave wet spots that turn into mold. A drying plan matches the number of air movers to the wet surfaces and the dehumidifier capacity to the room size and water load. Air movers are angled to create a circular airflow along wet walls. Dehumidifiers are placed so they can discharge water to a sink, tub or outside. The plan is adjusted each day based on readings, removing equipment from areas that are dry and focusing on areas that are not.

Drying After Category 2 and Category 3 Water

When water is contaminated, such as washing machine discharge, toilet overflows, sewage or flood water, cleaning and disinfecting come before drying. Blowing air across contaminated materials can spread bacteria and odors. Affected porous materials are removed first, surfaces are cleaned and treated, and only then does the drying system go in. Frequently Asked Questions About Structural Drying

Why can't I just open the windows to dry my home?

+

In Apopka, outdoor air is often more humid than indoor air, so open windows can actually add moisture. Closed-room dehumidification is much faster.

Why does the crew drill holes in my walls?

+

Small holes at the bottom of the wall, behind the baseboard, let dry air into the wall cavity so drywall and framing dry without being torn out.

How do I know my home is really dry?

+

Daily moisture readings compared against dry goals show when each material is dry. You should receive the readings with your job file.

Can drying prevent mold?

+

Yes. Fast, proper drying within 24 to 48 hours is the best way to prevent mold after water damage.

Will my electric bill go up during drying?

+

Slightly. The equipment uses electricity, and many insurers reimburse the added cost if you ask.
