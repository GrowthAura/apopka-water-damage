[HEADING_2] Can wet carpet in Apopka be saved or must it go?
[NORMAL_TEXT] Carpet wet with clean water and extracted within 24 to 48 hours can often be saved, but the pad is usually replaced. Carpet soaked by sewage or flood water must go.
[NORMAL_TEXT] Whether carpet can be saved depends on three things: the type of water, how long it has been wet, and the condition of the carpet. Clean water from a supply line or water heater, caught quickly, gives the best chance. In Apopka's humidity, mold and bacteria grow fast in carpet pad, so waiting even a couple of days changes the answer. Gray water from a washing machine or dishwasher usually means the pad goes and the carpet needs professional cleaning. Black water from sewage or outside flooding means both carpet and pad must be removed.
[HEADING_2] Floor by Floor: What Happens When It Gets Wet
[HEADING_3] Carpet and Pad
[NORMAL_TEXT] Carpet fibers dry fairly well, but the pad underneath holds water for days and is where odor and mold start. Carpet can delaminate, meaning the backing separates, if it stays wet too long. Tack strips along the walls can rot or rust.
[HEADING_3] Luxury Vinyl Plank (LVP)
[NORMAL_TEXT] LVP is popular in Apopka homes, especially newer builds, because the top layer is water-resistant. But it is not waterproof as an installed floor. Water slips through the seams and gets trapped between the planks and the slab or underlayment, where it cannot evaporate. LVP water damage often shows up as planks lifting, gapping or feeling soft, and a musty smell weeks after the leak.
[HEADING_3] Laminate
[NORMAL_TEXT] Laminate has a fiberboard core that swells and peaks at the seams when wet. Once swollen, it does not return to shape and usually needs replacement.
[HEADING_3] Engineered Hardwood
[NORMAL_TEXT] A thin layer of real wood over a plywood core. It handles brief moisture better than solid hardwood but can delaminate or cup if wet for long.
[HEADING_3] Solid Hardwood
[NORMAL_TEXT] Solid wood absorbs water and cups, with edges higher than the center, or crowns as it dries unevenly. Fast, controlled drying can often bring it back.
[HEADING_3] Ceramic and Porcelain Tile
[NORMAL_TEXT] Tile itself is waterproof, but grout and the thinset underneath are not. Tile floor water underneath can loosen tiles, leave hollow spots and keep the slab wet for a long time.

[HEADING_2] Wet Hardwood Floor Repair Apopka
[NORMAL_TEXT] Hardwood needs a different approach than carpet.
[NORMAL_TEXT] Standing water is extracted immediately with specialty wood floor tools.
[NORMAL_TEXT] Drying mats are placed over the wet boards to pull moisture up through the wood.

[HEADING_2] LVP Water Damage: Lift, Dry and Reinstall
[NORMAL_TEXT] LVP floating floors can often be saved by lifting the planks, drying the slab and underlayment, and reinstalling the same planks. That works when:
[NORMAL_TEXT] The planks are click-lock floating, not glued down.
[NORMAL_TEXT] The water was clean and caught quickly.
[NORMAL_TEXT] The planks are not warped, stained or delaminated.
[NORMAL_TEXT] When water was gray or black, or the underlayment is foam that held water, the underlayment is replaced and the planks are cleaned or replaced. Glued-down LVP is harder to save and often needs to be removed. Moisture testing the slab before reinstalling is critical; installing over a damp slab traps water again.

[HEADING_2] Tile Floor Water Underneath: Hidden Damage
[NORMAL_TEXT] Tile looks fine on top even when water is trapped below. Signs of water under tile include grout that darkens or stays damp, tiles that sound hollow when tapped, tiles that crack or pop up, and baseboards next to the tile that swell. Water that gets under tile through grout lines or from a slab leak can keep the slab and nearby walls wet for weeks. Moisture meters and thermal imaging show where water sits under tile. Often the tile can stay, with drying focused on walls and baseboards; loose or hollow tiles are reset after the slab dries.

[NORMAL_TEXT] Bedrooms in many Apopka homes are carpeted, and they often share walls with bathrooms, laundry rooms and the AC closet.
