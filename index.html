Why Water Damage in Apopka Behaves Differently

Here is what that means inside a typical Apopka home:

Concrete block on slab

Most homes have no basement or crawl space. Water spreads across the slab and wicks up into drywall, baseboards and door casings instead of draining away. Drying a slab takes patience and the right dehumidification.

Garage and interior water heaters

Manufactured home communities

Water Damage Categories: Clean, Gray and Black Water

The IICRC S500 standard sorts water losses into three categories, and the category decides how your home is cleaned.

Category 1, clean water

Comes from a sanitary source such as a burst supply pipe, water heater or ice-maker line. Usually the fastest to dry if handled quickly.

Category 2, gray water

Contains some contamination, such as washing machine discharge, dishwasher overflow or AC condensate that has sat in a pan. Needs cleaning and antimicrobial treatment.

Category 3, black water

Sewage, septic backups and outdoor flood water. Porous materials it touches usually have to be removed, and the area must be sanitized before drying.

Clean water does not stay clean. After about 48 hours in a warm Apopka home, Category 1 water can degrade into Category 2 or 3, which is one more reason to call right away.

Signs You Have Hidden Water Damage

Not every water problem announces itself with a puddle. Call for an inspection if you notice any of these:

  • A musty or earthy smell in a closet, bathroom or near the AC unit.
  • Brown rings, bubbling paint or soft spots on ceilings.
  • Baseboards that are swelling, separating or darker at the bottom.
  • Flooring that feels soft, warm or spongy, or tile that sounds hollow.
  • A water bill that jumped with no change in usage.
  • Condensation or water in the AC drain pan or around the air handler.
  • Warped door frames or doors that suddenly stick.

The earlier hidden water is found, the less needs to be torn out. Our leak detection and moisture mapping tools show what the naked eye cannot.

What to Do in the First Hour After Water Damage

The choices you make before the crew arrives make a real difference to the final outcome.

  • Shut off the water at the main valve or the fixture supply.
  • Turn off electricity to wet rooms at the breaker panel, and never stand in water near live outlets.
  • Take photos and video of everything before moving anything, for your insurance claim.
  • Lift furniture legs onto foil or wood blocks so stains do not transfer to wet carpet.
  • Move rugs, electronics, books and paperwork to a dry area.
  • Open interior doors to improve airflow, but keep windows closed if it is humid outside.
  • Do not use a household vacuum on standing water, and stay away from any water that may be sewage.
