[HEADING_2] How Professional Leak Detection Works
[HEADING_3] Thermal Imaging
[NORMAL_TEXT] A thermal imaging leak scan shows temperature differences on walls, floors and ceilings. Water evaporating from a wet area is cooler than dry surroundings, and hot water lines leaking under a slab show up as warm spots. Thermal imaging points to where to look closer, without opening anything.
[HEADING_3] Moisture Meters
[NORMAL_TEXT] Pin and pinless meters measure water content in drywall, baseboards, wood and flooring to confirm and map wet areas.
[HEADING_3] Acoustic Listening
[NORMAL_TEXT] Sensitive listening equipment picks up the sound of water escaping from a pressurized pipe, even through concrete or behind tile.
[HEADING_3] Pressure Testing
[NORMAL_TEXT] Sections of the plumbing system are isolated and pressurized to see which line is losing pressure, narrowing down the location.
[HEADING_3] Meter and Fixture Isolation
[NORMAL_TEXT] Shutting off fixtures one by one and watching the meter rules out toilets, irrigation and appliances.
[HEADING_3] Visual Inspection and Cameras
[NORMAL_TEXT] Under sinks, behind access panels, in the attic and around the water heater. Borescope cameras can look inside wall cavities through a small hole.

[HEADING_2] Irrigation and Outdoor Leaks in Apopka Yards
[NORMAL_TEXT] Most Apopka homes have in-ground irrigation systems, and they are one of the most common hidden sources of a high water bill. Broken heads, cracked lines from roots or lawn equipment, and valves that do not close fully can waste large amounts of water. Some homes in Apopka use reclaimed water for irrigation, which is metered separately, so check which meter is showing the extra use. Signs of an irrigation leak include soggy spots in the lawn, water running down the curb, patches of unusually green grass and sinkholes or depressions along the line. Running each zone while watching for leaks usually finds the problem.
[HEADING_2] Shower Pan and Tub Leaks
[NORMAL_TEXT] A leaking shower pan or tub drain is a common hidden leak in Florida homes. Because showers sit on the slab, water from a failed pan seeps into the slab and the walls around the shower, often showing up as damp carpet or swollen baseboards in the bedroom or closet next door. A simple test is to plug the drain and fill the shower base with an inch or two of water, mark the level and check it after several hours. A dropping level points to a pan leak. Grout and caulk failures around tile can also let water behind the walls.
[HEADING_2] Pool Leaks and Water Bills
[NORMAL_TEXT] Homes with pools sometimes see high water bills because the pool auto-fill keeps adding water to replace a leak. Losing more than about a quarter inch per day beyond normal evaporation is a sign of a leak. A bucket test, placing a bucket of water on the pool step and comparing the water levels after a day, helps tell evaporation from a leak. Pool leaks are handled by pool leak specialists, but if water from a pool leak is reaching the home's slab or walls, the moisture should be checked.
