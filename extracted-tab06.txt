[HEADING_2] What should you do first when a water heater leaks in Apopka?
[NORMAL_TEXT] Close the cold water valve above the tank, switch off power or gas to the heater, then shut the main water valve if water keeps flowing.
[NORMAL_TEXT] The cold supply valve is the pipe on top of the tank marked cold, usually with a lever or round handle. For an electric heater, flip its double breaker in the panel; for a gas unit, turn the gas control knob to off or pilot. If the valve will not close or the leak continues, shut the main valve where the water line enters the house. Then move anything you value out of the water and call the crew. Do not touch the tank or pipes if they are hot, and do not stand in water near an electric heater until the breaker is off.

[HEADING_2] Signs Your Water Heater Is About to Fail
[NORMAL_TEXT] Most tank failures give warnings before they burst. Watch for:
[NORMAL_TEXT] Rust on the bottom of the tank or around fittings.
[NORMAL_TEXT] Rusty or discolored hot water at the tap.
[NORMAL_TEXT] A puddle or dampness around the base, even a small one.
[NORMAL_TEXT] Water dripping from the temperature and pressure relief valve pipe.
[NORMAL_TEXT] Rumbling, popping or banging noises as the tank heats.
[NORMAL_TEXT] A tank more than 8 to 12 years old.
[NORMAL_TEXT] Water in the drain pan under the heater.
[NORMAL_TEXT] If you see any of these, have a plumber inspect or replace the unit before it floods your home.
[HEADING_2] Common Causes of Water Heater Burst Apopka FL Calls
[NORMAL_TEXT] Corroded tank: Over time the inner lining fails and the tank rusts through from the inside. This is the most common cause of a full tank failure.
[NORMAL_TEXT] Sediment buildup: Hard water minerals settle in the bottom, causing overheating and weak spots.
[NORMAL_TEXT] Failed T&P valve: A temperature and pressure relief valve that sticks open or leaks drips continuously.
[NORMAL_TEXT] Loose or corroded connections: Inlet and outlet fittings on top of the tank can leak slowly into the insulation and down the side.
[NORMAL_TEXT] Failed drain valve: The plastic drain valve near the bottom can crack or fail to seal.
[NORMAL_TEXT] No expansion tank: In homes with a check valve or pressure regulator, thermal expansion with no expansion tank puts extra stress on the tank.
[HEADING_2] T&P Valve Leaking vs Tank Failure: Know the Difference
[NORMAL_TEXT] A T&P valve leaking is usually a slow drip or periodic discharge from the relief pipe. It is often caused by high pressure or temperature, or a worn valve. It still causes water damage if the pipe drains into a closet or under the floor, so call a plumber.
[NORMAL_TEXT] A tank failure is a steady flow from the bottom or side of the tank itself. The tank cannot be repaired, only replaced, and the water damage needs immediate extraction.
[NORMAL_TEXT] In both cases, if water reached drywall, carpet or flooring, it needs to be dried properly so it does not cause mold.

[HEADING_2] Preventing Your Next Water Heater Flood
[NORMAL_TEXT] Flush the tank once a year to remove sediment, especially with Central Florida's hard water.
[NORMAL_TEXT] Test the T&P valve annually.
[NORMAL_TEXT] Check the anode rod every few years and replace it when it is worn.
[NORMAL_TEXT] Install a drain pan with a drain line, especially for closet units.
[NORMAL_TEXT] Add a water leak sensor or automatic shut-off valve next to the heater.
[NORMAL_TEXT] Plan to replace tank heaters around 10 to 12 years of age.
[NORMAL_TEXT] Make sure everyone in the house knows where the cold supply valve is.
[HEADING_2] Tank vs Tankless Water Heater Leaks
[HEADING_3] Tank Heaters
[NORMAL_TEXT] Most Apopka homes still use a 40 or 50 gallon tank. When a tank fails, it can release its full volume plus whatever keeps flowing from the supply line until the water is shut off. That is why the cold supply valve matters so much.
[HEADING_3] Tankless Heaters
[NORMAL_TEXT] Tankless units hold very little water, so they rarely flood a room on their own. Leaks usually come from fittings, the pressure relief valve or the condensate line on high-efficiency gas models. These are slow leaks that soak the wall behind the unit over time.
[HEADING_3] Hybrid Heat Pump Heaters
[NORMAL_TEXT] Heat pump water heaters are growing in popularity in Central Florida because of the warm climate. Like an air conditioner, they produce condensate that drains through a small line. If that line clogs, water overflows onto the floor, much like an AC leak.
