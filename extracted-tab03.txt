[HEADING_2] Polybutylene and CPVC in Apopka: Should You Repipe?
[NORMAL_TEXT] Polybutylene was widely used in Florida homes built in the late 1970s through the mid-1990s. It is gray, sometimes blue or black, and stamped PB2110. Over time, chlorine in municipal water breaks it down from the inside, and fittings fail without warning. Many insurers ask about it, and some will not write a policy on a home that still has it.
[NORMAL_TEXT] CPVC, the cream or tan plastic pipe common in later homes, is more durable but grows brittle with age and heat, especially in attics. It can crack when bumped during repairs or AC work.
[NORMAL_TEXT] If your Apopka home still has polybutylene, talk to a plumber about repiping. It is far less expensive than repeated water damage, and it can make your home easier to insure. If you are not sure what you have, look at the pipes coming out of the water heater or under a sink.
